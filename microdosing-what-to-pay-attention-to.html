Microdosing Mushrooms: What You Should Actually Pay Attention To

The mushrooms don’t always teach the lesson you were hoping for. Here’s what I pay attention to before, during, and after.

Microdosing 01 · ~5 min watch · Branches: Catalysts & Expanded States
Full Transcript Click any line to jump

If you’re going to microdose, I want to help you get as much out of it as possible. We’re going to talk about when to take it, how strong you want it to feel, what to pair it with, how to set an intention, what to do when something challenging comes up, how to capture the insights, and how to tell if microdosing is actually helping you over time. I’ll give you three of the most common microdosing protocols, plus the more intuitive approach that I personally use, and much more.

Let’s get into it. The first thing I tell anyone about microdosing is learn how it feels for you. There isn’t one perfect way to do this. Same amount can feel different depending upon the mushrooms, their strength, and the person taking them. Different protocols work for different people, and what feels right on one day might not be what you want on another. Listen to your body. Learn what fits into your life because nobody knows you better than you do.

Personally, I love microdosing spending time outside, going to a concert, having an introspective morning, being creative, or enjoying time with a friend. Sometimes when I feel a little off course, I’ll work with them as well. They can help bring back some of the joy in life, get me out of my head, bring me back into the present moment. Breath work, meditation, yoga, gratitude, and anything else that helps you become more mindful and put in for yourself can pair beautifully with microdosing.

Timing matters, too. I really love mornings. There’s something clear and crisp about starting the day that way. When you’re first figuring out how microdosing affects you, pick a day where you don’t have too many obligations or anything heavy you need to deal with. Give yourself some time to experience it and see how it feels. Food can change the experience, too. In my experience, taking it around a meal can make it feel a little bit lighter. And be mindful of taking it around bedtime. Microdosing can be very energizing. So, I personally give myself at least 4 hours before going to sleep.

Strength is another big one. Not all mushrooms are the same strength. Different strains of different batches can affect you differently. Personally, I like mine where I can feel them a little bit while still having all of my focus and clarity, but you can also make them much lighter, even sub-perceptual, where you’re not consciously feeling them at all. Personally, I also like making my own microdoses. There are plenty of videos out there that show you how to do it, so I’m not going to turn this into that video. But, I like knowing exactly what’s going into mine, being able to keep the strength and the blend consistent. I also add several non-psychoactive mushrooms to my blend such as lion’s mane for some extra focus and clarity. Another thing that can be really powerful is setting an intention.

Maybe there’s something you want to understand, something you’ve been thinking about, a direction you’re considering. You can bring that into the experience and spend some time with it. That doesn’t mean the mushrooms are going to take you exactly where you expected. One thing I’ve learned with psychedelics is sometimes they give you the lesson you need, not the one you wanted.

Microdosing can also bring up what we call shadow work. These are often parts of ourselves we’ve pushed away, forgotten, or simply haven’t been able to see clearly. An emotion might surface, a memory, a pattern you suddenly recognize.

Some of it can feel uncomfortable, but it can also give you an opportunity to actually look at what’s there. And when something important comes through, get it out of your head, write it down, talk it through with someone, or capture it through voice to text. I use a process called vision writing where I simply speak what’s coming through and I capture it as I go.

I have a full video explaining that process if you want to learn how to do it. Another really useful tip is to conduct your own citizen science. Before you start, take a little inventory. How’s your mood, your anxiety, your substance use, your relationships, creativity, whatever actually matters to you. Then see what changes over time. And don’t change too many variables at once. Give each enough time that you can actually understand what it’s doing for you because microdosing is very subtle. So I don’t like mixing a lot of other substances in with it. Alcohol can really dull the experience rather than add to it. Now let’s talk about protocols. There are three common approaches you’ll hear about a lot.

The Fadiman protocol is one day on followed by 2 days off. Then you repeat the cycle. The Microdosing Institute protocol is every other day. One day on, one day off. And the Stamets protocol is four days on followed by three days off. Those structures can be helpful if you like having a clear framework.

Personally, I work with microdosing more intuitively. Some weeks I might microdose a few times, other weeks I won’t at all. Sometimes I’ll go longer without feeling called toward it. I look at what I’m doing, how I’m feeling, and whether it seems like something that belongs in my day.

And that brings us back to where we started. Nobody knows you better than you do. Experience it for yourself. Be mindful of any changes you notice. If it helps, beautiful. If it doesn’t, that’s okay, too. Keep the tools that move your needle in the right direction and leave the rest behind.

Overview

Nobody knows you better than you do.

One of the biggest things I’ve learned is to pay attention to your own experience rather than assuming someone else’s approach automatically belongs in your life. The same amount can feel different depending on the mushrooms and the person taking them.

One of the most important parts for me is what happens after something comes through. An emotion, memory, pattern, or idea doesn’t mean much if you never work with it. Write it down. Talk it through. Give yourself enough space to see whether something is actually changing in your life.
