How to Become Fully Present Using What You See, Hear & Feel

A deep dive into See, Hear, Feel — using your senses to come fully into the moment, and noticing the awareness that’s present underneath everything you experience.

How you turn almost any moment of your life into a meditation. You start by reconnecting to your senses and actually noticing the life happening around you and within you. As I was writing this, I was sitting on my deck watching a ladybug walk in circles over and over. I couldn’t tell if it was dancing to the music or if its left blinker was stuck in the on position, but I noticed it. This tiny little thing that I could have easily missed had my complete attention. And there was something beautiful and perfect in that simplicity. That’s where this practice begins. There’s a difference between looking at something and really seeing it. Between a sound reaching your ears and actually listening to it, between knowing that you feel something and being connected enough to notice. How is it moving through your body? Most of these experiences, they move right past us without us ever really noticing them.

Our fractured attention, it’s already moved on to something else. One of the teachers I’ve learned the most from Shinzen Young developed a simple practice for this called see, hear, and feel. The basic idea is to really see what you are seeing. Truly hear what you’re hearing and feel what is happening within and around your body. The wind moving across your face. A bird singing outside. A memory passing through your mind. A feeling taking shape in you. Simply notice what is there without immediately putting a good or a bad connotation on it. Everything becomes part of the practice. If this feels a little unfamiliar at first, that’s okay. You’re developing a skill and like anything else, it becomes more natural with practice. You don’t have to sit down and close your eyes to experience this. Your entire life itself becomes the meditation. At the end of this video, I’m going to guide you through the practice so you can experience it for yourself. But first, let’s look more deeply at seeing, hearing, and feeling because there’s so much more happening within each of them than we normally realize.

Let’s start with seeing. Seeing includes everything you see in the world around you, but it also includes anything you see within your mind. You might watch clouds moving across the sky. You might also close your eyes and see a place you haven’t been to for years, or the face of someone you love. One is happening in front of you, the other is happening within your mind. But both are part of seeing. Whether the image is in front of you or inside your mind, the practice is the same. When something catches your attention, stay with it. Slow down. Really notice every little detail. Most of the time, we recognize what something is and move on before we’ve ever really seen it. I saw a ladybug. My mind said ladybug. And that could have been the end of it. But the word ladybug does not show you the shine on its red shell. All of its tiny little legs moving in unison or the strange little circles it keeps making across the deck. All those details were there the entire time. I just had to slow down enough to see them. You don’t have to go looking for something extraordinary. You’re just learning to see how much is already happening around you in the things you normally think of as ordinary.

Now, I want you to hear every sound happening around you. Whoa, that caught me off guard. I bet it caught your attention, too. Right as I started working on this section, a train horn went off several times. It was incredibly loud, and it completely pulled me away from what I was doing. But when it stopped, the silence afterward felt so much deeper. Even the absence of sound becomes part of what you’re hearing. Hearing includes every sound happening around you, but it also includes everything you hear within your mind. The wind moving through the trees, a person’s voice, music playing. Hearing is not only about what a sound is. It can tell you where it is. A woodpecker somewhere off in the distance. How far away is it? Which direction is it coming from? Does it stay in one place or is it moving? Without even looking, you can begin to sense the whole space around you. But hearing can also be your internal monologue, an impactful conversation from years ago that you can still hear as clearly as it was happening right now. Even though no sound is coming from the outside world, you’re still hearing it. A sound can reach your ears without ever really reaching you. Truly listening begins when you give it your full attention, thinking about a song you’ve heard hundreds of times. Then one day, a lyric you’ve sung for years takes on a completely new meaning. Or hear a sound in the music that you’ve never noticed before. The song didn’t change, your attention did. Then turn on that same attention inward. When the voice inside your head begins speaking, simply listen to it without following it. You don’t need perfect silence to practice this. Sound doesn’t have to be beautiful for this practice. Let any sound be sound and simply listen.

The more deeply you listen, the more of your life you actually experience. Now, I want you to try something with me. Take a second and smile. Even if you’re not in the mood for it, just just come with me. Just let a smile move across your face. But this is more than just making the expression. Let the smile reach your whole face. Feel the strength in your cheeks, the corners of your mouth rising, slight squint in your eyes. You can actually feel your energy rise as you smile. And the more you fully embody it, the more noticeable that shift becomes. That’s one of the beautiful things about feeling. Even a small shift in your body can change the way the entire moment feels or your entire day. Feeling includes everything happening within your body. Everything you experience across it. The warmth of the sun, the ground underneath your feet, the breath moving through you within this practice. Taste and smell are also included under feeling because you experience both of those through your body. And sometimes one experience reaches you through several senses at once. You can watch the clouds rolling across the sky, hear the wind moving through the trees, feel the cool air across your skin, and smell the rain being carried in on the breeze. All those pieces come together giving you a much fuller experience of the moment.

Feeling also includes your emotions because emotions are experienced within the body. Excitement might feel like energy rising through you. Sadness might feel heavy. Anxiety might show up as tightness in your chest or tension in your stomach. Before you name the emotion or decide what it means, feel what’s physically happening. Thoughts and feelings can also create loops together. A thought can change the way your body feels. And that feeling can pull you even deeper into the thought. You don’t have to suppress any of it or immediately turn it into something good or bad. Simply feel what is happening clearly enough that it no longer controls the entire experience from underneath the surface.

Now take a moment and check in with your body. Notice your jaw, your shoulders, your hands. Is there any tension you’re holding? I’m sure there is. Don’t Don’t change it immediately. Feel it first. Then see if intention alone can let some of that tension release. Relax your jaw. Let your shoulders drop. Open your hands. Sit a little taller. Roll your thumbs out. It can kind of drop your shoulders. And notice what changes. This awareness is something you’re building. And it doesn’t have to end there. Once you can feel what your body is doing, you can become an active participant in your own experience. Notice what’s there. Then notice what you’re able to shift. The more connected you become to feeling, the easier it is to recognize what lifts you, what drains you, and what feels right for you. Your body is communicating with you all day long. Seeing, hearing, and feeling often happen all at once.

But to experience something fully, your attention generally has to rest on one thing at a time. Let your attention move freely between them. Whatever becomes clearest, give it your full attention. As you practice, you become better at staying with what you notice, perceiving more of what is actually there, and meeting it without actually deciding immediately if it’s good or bad.

But to experience any of this, you have to create enough space to notice it. And sometimes that means turning down everyone else’s channel long enough to watch your own. Stillness is an important part of this practice. But stillness doesn’t mean you have to sit silently or stopping everything you’re doing. It can be as simple as giving one ordinary moment your full attention. Take a barefoot walk through the grass. Simply feel the ground beneath your feet. Hear the leaves moving around you. Notice the light shifting through the trees. Or take a Sunday drive with the windows down. Hear the wind moving around the car. Feel it rushing across your skin as the world passes you by. Even while your body is moving, something within you can become still. This is what I mean by watching your own channel. You’re simply giving yourself time to notice what the moment actually feels like. Sometimes stillness means giving another person your full attention. You see the expression on their face, hear the tone of their voice, and notice what you feel as you listen. In that space, life has room to surprise you. You often notice something beautiful.

Feel an unexpected connection or experience a moment of clarity. And beneath everything you see, hear, and feel, you may begin to notice something even deeper. The awareness experiencing all of it. Your senses are simply the doorway. Awareness is what notices them. If you can notice a thought moving through your mind or notice a feeling within your body, there has to be a deeper awareness noticing those things. A thought appears, you know it’s there, then it passes, but the awareness remains. The same thing happens with a sound, an image or a physical sensation. They move through your experience, but something within you is here to experience all of them. At first, you notice what you see, hear, and feel, but the practice deepens. Begin to notice the space between them, the silence between the sounds, the stillness beneath your thoughts, the quiet underneath everything you feel. That space is not empty. It is awareness itself. And this is where for me the practice becomes more than simply paying attention. You begin to recognize the eternal soul beneath all of it. The part of you connected to something much deeper to the underlying intelligence moving through all of life. You can call it consciousness, spirit, soul or something greater. However you understand it, it is something you have to feel for yourself. At first you become more aware of your life. Eventually, you become more aware of the awareness itself. One sense may come more naturally to you than the others, but your attention will it’s going to wander.

And that’s okay. When you notice it, just come back to something you can see, hear, or feel. That returning is the practice. Those repetitions slowly rewire your brain, the way you experience your life. So, instead of talking about it any longer, let’s experience it together.

Take a moment and let your body settle wherever you are. Take one slow deep breath in and let it go with a long exhale. Keeping your eyes open, look around you. See what your eyes are naturally drawn to. Really focus your attention and actually see it. Notice its color, its shape, its texture, how the light falls across it. Now listen for whatever sound pulls your attention towards it. Is it close to you? Somewhere in the distance? Stay with that sound and truly listen. Now, let’s bring your attention into your body. Notice the weight of your body against the chair or the ground beneath you. Notice your breath. Feel the air moving in and out through your nostrils and filling your lungs. Follow the breath as it moves back out. Notice whether your body feels relaxed or tense, heavy or light, and see if there’s any emotion or energy moving within you. Now allow seeing, hearing, and feeling to all become part of the same moment. Let one thing come forward, give it your full attention. When something else becomes clearer, let your attention rest there. Nothing needs to be changed. Nothing needs to be judged. Just experience what is here. Now notice that you are aware of what you see, hear, and feel. But the deeper question is, can you notice the awareness itself? The stillness underneath all of it. Take one more slow deep breath with me, in, and out. Let go of any remaining tension. Notice that nothing around you had to change. You simply tuned into your own channel and became more aware of the life happening around you and within you.

That’s how almost any moment becomes a meditation. This was only a taste of the practice. I’ve created a longer guided see, hear, feel experience you can return to anytime. Click the video on the screen and we’ll go deeper into the practice together. Your life is already happening. See it, hear it, feel it.

Chapters
Overview

Your senses are simply the doorway. Awareness is what notices them.

Most of life passes through our senses without us ever fully noticing it. There’s a difference between looking at something and really seeing it, between a sound reaching your ears and actually listening to it.

See, Hear, Feel is a practice from Shinzen Young, one of the teachers I’ve learned the most from. Really see what you are seeing, truly hear what you’re hearing, and feel what’s happening within and around your body. As the practice deepens, you begin to notice the awareness underneath all of it.

Main Ideas

Seeing, outside and in

Hearing, outside and in

The wind in the trees, and your internal monologue. Even the silence after a train horn becomes part of what you hear.

Feeling includes emotions

0404

Feel it, then shift it

Notice the tension in your jaw and shoulders. Then see if intention alone can let some of it release.

0505

Watch your own channel

Sometimes you have to turn down everyone else’s channel long enough to watch your own.

0606

The awareness itself

If you can notice a thought or a feeling, there has to be a deeper awareness noticing it.
