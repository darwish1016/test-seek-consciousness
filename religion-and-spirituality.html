Spirituality — Inner Authority

Religion & Spirituality: What Did We Get Wrong?

How do we know what’s actually true about God, and what’s a fabrication of mankind?

Spirituality 02 · ~10.5 min watch · Roots: Inner Authority
Full Transcript Click any line to jump

How do we know what’s actually true about God and what’s a fabrication of mankind? The point of religion and spirituality is to connect us to something greater. God is love. But when those teachings become about fear, control, who’s right or who’s wrong, what are they actually connecting us to? We’re going to look at where these teachings came from, what mankind has added along the way, and what might be standing between you and your own connection to God.

Religion can give us teachings, practices, and community. Spirituality is the actual lived connection with spirit, God, or source. And they can work together beautifully. From what I’ve experienced, our own spirit and God or source aren’t actually two separate things. We’re all individual expressions of the same spirit experiencing this life through our own unique lens. So what is your religion or spirituality actually doing for you? Is it helping you experience that connection more deeply? And is that connection changing the way you live?

Every religion started somewhere. Someone or group had a profound spiritual experience, connected with something greater, and wanted to share what they discovered with others. But think about what they’re trying to do. They’re trying to explain something infinite through a finite human mind. And how do you even begin to do that? Words are limited. You can describe what happened, what you felt, what you understood, but you can’t fully explain an experience like that. They’re so profound. It has to be felt for yourself. And even when someone believes they received a message directly from God, they’re still interpreting that experience through their own understanding. Over time, those interpretations become teachings, traditions, and entire religious systems. But we’re human beings.

We have egos, fears, beliefs, and biases. And those things become part of what we pass down, too. So, how much of what we’ve been taught about God actually comes from that original connection? And how much has mankind added along the way? Think about a child when they’re born. I believe we come into this world pure, innocent, and loving. We’re not born into believing we’re sinners or that there’s something wrong with us. But as we grow up, we begin taking on all these different beliefs, fears, and misunderstandings from the people around us. Our parents pass down what they learned from their parents, often with the very best intentions, sometimes not. And religion can work the same way. Many of us were taught what to believe about God long before we were even old enough to question it. But not everything we’re taught is actually true. And not everything we’ve inherited brings us closer to ourselves or to God.

From my own experience connecting with God, what I experienced was this pure, infinite, radiant, intelligent love. God has no bias. God loves you regardless of where you were born, your religion, or what you’ve been taught to believe. God loves you all the same. I believe we’re here to have the freedom to choose, to create, and to experience this life in our own unique ways. Our choices still have consequences, of course. But that’s different from believing God stops loving us when we make a mistake. Wars fought in the name of religion have cost millions of lives. But really think about that. Would God want us killing people God created because they believe something different? I don’t know. I believe our image of a punishing God is mankind’s own fear being projected onto God. How is any of that bringing us closer to love?

And we need to look at what religion has been used for as well. It’s been used to control populations, reinforce power, and exploit people financially to shape what people believe, how they live, and who they’re supposed to follow. And when those things are presented as what God wants, people’s fear can become a way of keeping them under someone else’s control. Are these teachings helping you connect to something greater? Or is someone using your desire for that connection to gain power over you? But that isn’t what our connection to God is for. Spirituality is learning to love each other more deeply. If something is truly aligned with spirit, it should be the greatest good for all of us. So when you look at what’s being taught, who is it actually serving? I mean, if God is infinite, why would that connection only be accessible through one human system of belief? It doesn’t make sense to me.

From what I’ve experienced, we’re all one. We all share one consciousness. It’s the same spirit underneath all of us. We all have the same access to God or source, regardless of our religion, race, education, or beliefs. But look at all the divisions we’ve created. This religion is right, this one’s wrong. One group supposedly has greater access to God than another. And we can do the same thing in spirituality. We begin seeing ourselves as more enlightened, more conscious, or more connected than somebody else. But if we’re all one, how could anyone be spiritually superior to anyone else?

And that’s where ego can sneak right back in. We start exploring spirituality, having profound experiences, learning all these different things, and without even realizing it, we can begin building an entire identity around being spiritual. And this is something I’ve had to examine in my own practice, too. Because even when our intention is to connect more deeply, we can end up using spirituality to strengthen the very ego we’re trying to move beyond. But spirituality is about connecting to what we are underneath the ego. And I think that’s worth examining in our own lives. Are we using these experiences to connect more deeply or using them to become somebody?

Think about two people sitting next to each other saying the exact same prayer. One might feel a profound connection to God while the other might not feel much of anything. Same words, same practice, completely different experience. And neither person is necessarily doing anything wrong. You can know every passage in a sacred text and recite it perfectly. You can attend church every Sunday or sit in ceremony after ceremony, but if none of it actually shifts anything within you, what have you really gained? These teachings and practices can be incredibly valuable. But knowing something and experiencing it are two different things. And that’s what I want you to recognize.

It doesn’t matter how religious or spiritual your life looks on the outside. What matters is what’s actually happening within you. Religion has some beautiful pieces. Prayer, contemplation, chanting, music, and there are countless other ways to connect through meditation, yoga, nature, etc. But just because you find something valuable in a spiritual tradition doesn’t mean you have to accept every part of it. For some people, everything they need might be part of one religion. For others, their path might come from many different traditions or things they’ve discovered on their own. Spirituality is of course unique for each of us. You can explore these different pieces. Try them for yourself and choose what belongs in your own spiritual path. You can have the most profound spiritual experience of your life.

But what happens when you come back to your everyday world. Does it change how you love yourself, how you treat other people, or how you see mother nature and the world around you? Because spirituality isn’t just about having these incredible experiences. It’s about actually embodying what you’ve discovered and bringing that love into your life. And it’s not just for the beautiful moments. When life gets really difficult, having that deeper connection can help keep you afloat. It gives you something to hold on to, a light you can find even in some of your darkest moments. I’d like you to take a real honest inventory of where you’re at with your religion, your spirituality, and your own spirit.

What would you like that connection to become? And what are you willing to put into it? Take a look at what might be getting in your way. Maybe there are some beliefs you’ve never questioned or habits and distractions that are taking you further away from that connection. What could you let go of? And I’d encourage you to try one new spiritual practice. Maybe something you wouldn’t normally explore. Give it a real chance for a month and see what it brings into your life. If it’s something worthwhile, keep it. If not, try something else. Please share how this relates to your own beliefs and experiences in the comments, including where you see things differently.

We all have something we can learn from each other. Mankind has created countless ways of exploring God, but we didn’t create that connection, and that’s important. It’s eternal. It’s always been and it always will be. Underneath all the fears, beliefs, and interpretations we’ve accumulated, that pure infinite love is still there. And the more we connect with that love, the more of it we can share with the world.

I’ve felt what it’s like to become one with God or source. And nothing I’ve ever felt has been more profound or beautiful. It changed my entire understanding of life. That’s actually why I’m sitting here talking to you. I don’t want you to simply believe my story. I want you to discover that connection for yourself. We spent so much of our lives learning about what other people believe about God. But how much time have you actually spent experiencing that for yourself?

Overview

Religion can give us teachings, practices, and community. Spirituality is the actual lived connection with spirit, God, or source. They can work together beautifully. But over time, human fears, biases, and desires for control can become mixed into the teachings.

From what I’ve experienced, God is pure, infinite, radiant, intelligent love. You don’t have to accept everything you’ve been taught. Explore it. Question it. And give yourself the opportunity to experience that connection directly.
